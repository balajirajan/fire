What is body fat percentage?

Body fat percentage is the proportion of your total body weight that's made up of fat, as opposed to muscle, bone, organs and water (your "lean mass"). It's a more useful fitness marker than weight or BMI alone, since two people at the same weight and height can have very different amounts of fat versus muscle.

How is this calculated?

This calculator uses the U.S. Navy circumference method, developed by the U.S. Navy as a fast, equipment-free alternative to skinfold calipers or a DEXA scan. It uses your neck and waist measurements (plus hip, for women) along with your height.

Men: Body Fat % = 495 / (1.0324 − 0.19077 × log₁₀(waist − neck) + 0.15456 × log₁₀(height)) − 450

Women: Body Fat % = 495 / (1.29579 − 0.35004 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100 × log₁₀(height)) − 450

How is the calorie deficit calculated?

Roughly 7,700 kcal of energy deficit corresponds to 1 kg of fat loss. This calculator first estimates your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula (based on your age, sex, height, weight and activity level), then works out how many days it'll take to reach your target weight at your chosen daily deficit.

  • Total calories to burn = (current weight − target weight) × 7,700 kcal
  • Days needed = total calories to burn ÷ daily deficit
  • Daily calorie target = your TDEE − daily deficit

Is a 500 kcal/day deficit safe?

A deficit of 500 kcal/day is the most commonly recommended starting point - it targets roughly 0.5 kg of fat loss per week, which is considered sustainable for most adults. Deficits above 1,000 kcal/day, or a daily target below 1,200 kcal (women) / 1,500 kcal (men), are generally not recommended without medical supervision - this calculator will flag it if your inputs land there.

This tool provides estimates for general wellness purposes only, using widely published formulas (U.S. Navy method, Mifflin-St Jeor). It is not a medical device and does not replace advice from a doctor, dietitian, or other qualified professional - especially if you have an existing health condition or are pregnant.
